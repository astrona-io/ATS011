A `ClusterPolicy` rule that matches `kind: Pod` looks like it only ever sees bare Pods. In practice, almost nobody applies a bare Pod — Deployments, StatefulSets, DaemonSets, Jobs, and CronJobs all create Pods on your behalf, wrapping the Pod spec inside a `spec.template` (or, for a CronJob, `spec.jobTemplate.spec.template`). If Kyverno only checked literal `kind: Pod` admissions, a Pod-matching policy would be nearly useless in a real cluster — everyone would just wrap their non-compliant Pod spec in a Deployment and sail straight past it.

That gap is closed by **autogen**: Kyverno detects that a rule's `match` selects `Pod`, and — unless you've told it not to — clones the rule, rewrites its field paths to reach through each controller's Pod template, and retargets the clone at `Deployment`, `DaemonSet`, `StatefulSet`, `Job`, `ReplicaSet`, `ReplicationController`, and `CronJob`. Your one rule becomes several, automatically, without you writing a single extra line of YAML.

This module covers exactly what autogen produces and how to control it.

## How this module is organised

1. **[Part 1 — What Autogen Actually Generates](./course-01-what-autogen-generates.md)** — applying a Pod-matching rule, finding the real autogenerated rules Kyverno produced, and proving a non-compliant Deployment gets rejected by a rule that only ever said `Pod`.
2. **[Part 2 — Controlling Autogen's Scope](./course-02-controlling-autogen-scope.md)** — the `pod-policies.kyverno.io/autogen-controllers` annotation, disabling generation entirely with `none`, and why narrowing the scope is not the same as granting an exemption.
3. **[Part 3 — When Autogen Declines, and When It Gets It Wrong](./course-03-when-autogen-declines.md)** — the multi-kind `match` that produces nothing at all, the pattern that produces a confidently useless rule, why declining is the safer of Kyverno's options, and how to check rather than assume.

## Learning objectives

After this module you can:

- Explain, in your own words, what field-path rewriting autogen performs when it clones a Pod rule for a Deployment versus a CronJob.
- Find the live autogenerated rules on an applied `ClusterPolicy` using `kubectl get clusterpolicy -o yaml`.
- Predict, before testing it, whether a given non-compliant Deployment, Job, or CronJob will be rejected by a Pod-only rule.
- Use the `pod-policies.kyverno.io/autogen-controllers` annotation to scope autogen to specific controller kinds, or turn it off entirely.
- Recognize the situations where autogen does not fire at all (a multi-kind `match`) versus situations where it fires but produces something you need to double-check (a pattern that already anticipates a template-shaped path).

## Before you start

You should already be comfortable writing a `validate.pattern` rule against `kind: Pod` (Section 010) and reading the rejection errors Kyverno returns on `kubectl apply`. You do not need anything from later sections — this module only concerns itself with what happens to a Pod rule once Pod-controller kinds enter the picture.

The linked lab gives you a `kind` cluster with Kyverno already installed and running. You will write a Pod-only `ClusterPolicy` yourself, then prove it also protects a Deployment without you ever mentioning `kind: Deployment`.
